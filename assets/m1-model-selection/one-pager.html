Model Selection One-Pager

The four levers, the six model classes, and the back-of-the-napkin decision flow. Pin it up. Names rotate; classes are stable.

The four levers

1 · Parameter count vs effective intelligence Bigger is not always better. A 30B reasoning-tuned model often beats a 70B chat-tuned model on structured output and code. Reasoning training matters more than raw size at the margins that affect your work.
2 · Context window vs cost You pay per token in and out. Frontier models are genuinely good at finding one fact in 200K tokens; reasoning and synthesis across the whole window fall off well before the advertised limit. Measure on your own task.
3 · Quantization vs quality Q4 is fine for chat. Q8 or fp16 for code, structured output, or anything where one wrong token tanks the result. On hosted APIs this is the provider's problem; it becomes your lever when you run open models yourself.
4 · Latency vs throughput Streaming chat needs first-token latency under 500ms or it feels broken. Batch jobs care about tokens-per-second-per-dollar instead. Different model classes win in each regime.

The decision flow (back of the napkin)

  1. Is the input an image, audio, or video? → Multimodal. Audio/video narrows the field further; every frontier model reads images.
  2. Is the output mostly code? → Code-specialized (or frontier reasoning when a subtle bug is expensive).
  3. Is it classification, routing, or simple chat where latency matters? → Small efficient. Cheapest and fastest; wrong answers are cheap to catch.
  4. Does a wrong answer cost more than the API call? → Frontier reasoning. Pay for the thinking when the stakes justify it.
  5. Everything else (synthesis, long context, balanced cost) → Mid-size open. The workhorse tier.

Two standing rules. Model names go stale within 6 months; the classes are what you memorize. And benchmark scores are gamed and often misleading: your eval set, on your data, decides which specific model wins inside a class.
